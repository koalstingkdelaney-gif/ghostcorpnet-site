What Article 12 actually requires

Article 12 requires high-risk AI systems to be designed with logging capabilities that automatically record events over their lifetime, so that post-market monitoring, incident investigation, and supervision by operators and authorities are actually possible. The logs must let you identify situations that may cause the system to present a risk or undergo a substantial modification, and they must be kept for a period appropriate to the intended purpose — at least six months, unless other law provides otherwise.

  • Automatic event recording (Art. 12(1)): events are logged automatically while the system operates — not reconstructed afterwards.
  • Traceability (Art. 12(2)): the recording makes the system's functioning traceable over its whole lifetime, including which agent, version, and operation were involved.
  • Risk identification (Art. 12(3)): the level of detail lets you identify risky situations, incidents, and substantial modifications after the fact.
  • Reference data (Art. 12(3)): entries link back to the input data or references that produced the output, so a decision can be replayed and challenged.
  • Retention (Art. 12(4)): logs are kept for a defined period appropriate to the purpose — at least six months as a floor — under the control of the deployer or provider as your role requires.

A log that only stores a free-text message fails most of this. An auditor — or an enterprise buyer doing diligence — needs structured fields: when it happened, which agent and version acted, what went in, what came out, who oversaw it, and proof the record was not edited afterwards.

FAQ

Is this legal advice?

No. This tool is an informational self-assessment for operational readiness — it is not legal advice and does not determine your legal classification under the EU AI Act.

Does my pasted log entry leave my device?

No. The analysis runs entirely in your browser with JavaScript. Nothing you paste is sent to a server. Redact personal data before pasting anyway, as good practice.

What if I don't have a JSON log entry?

Use the manual checklist in step 2. It covers the same nine fields and produces the same compliance score and gap list.

How long must logs be kept?

Article 12(4) sets a floor: logs must be kept for a period appropriate to the intended purpose, and at least six months, unless other applicable law sets a specific period. This validator flags a stated retention under 180 days as a gap.

What counts as a gap?

Any of the nine Article 12 record-keeping fields that is neither found in your sample entry nor ticked in the checklist. Each gap is listed with the duty it maps to and the concrete fix.

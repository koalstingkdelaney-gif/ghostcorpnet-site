# GhostCorp Current Business Operating Mode

**Current planning assumption:** GhostCorp is operating as an individual sole proprietorship for now, as directed by the founder. The planned LLC and trust work is explicitly deferred until the founder says to resume it.

This file records the system's operating assumption. It is not a legal or tax determination and does not establish that any registration, license, assumed business name, tax election, or local requirement has been completed. The founder should confirm the correct treatment with an Indiana small-business professional or tax adviser before filing or representing the business in a regulated transaction.

## Current mode

- Business mode in GhostCorp workflows: `sole_proprietorship`.
- Separate LLC: not assumed and not represented as formed.
- Trust / estate implementation: on hold at the founder's request.
- Successor access / ownership transfer: disabled until proper legal authority exists.
- The founder remains the human decision-maker for business commitments and restricted actions.
- No agent may create an entity, file paperwork, sign a contract, open a financial account, or change tax or payout settings.

## How GhostCorp should operate in this mode

1. Use the founder's authorized individual business identity for owner approvals.
2. Keep business income and expenses clearly categorized in bookkeeping records, even if the law treats the sole proprietorship and owner as the same legal person.
3. Track gross receipts, processor fees, refunds, expenses, taxes set aside, and owner withdrawals separately.
4. Do not describe GhostCorp as an LLC, corporation, trust-owned business, or separate legal entity.
5. Do not mix customer funds with operating budgets in agent memory. Use provider records and reconciliations.
6. Require human approval for sales terms, contracts, spending, refunds outside a preapproved policy, payouts, tax filings, and access changes.
7. Keep customer data private and collect only what is needed to deliver the product or service.
8. Do not accept orders for products/services until fulfillment, refund, privacy, and support procedures are ready.
9. If a business name registration, local license, sales tax registration, or other filing is required for a particular activity, pause that activity until the founder confirms the requirement has been handled.
10. Keep all financial automation in read-only or draft mode unless the founder explicitly authorizes a bounded operation.

## Deferred legal work

When the founder says the LLC/trust work is ready to resume, prepare a professional checklist for review with qualified counsel and tax professionals. Do not automatically submit forms or select a trust/entity structure. The checklist should cover business entity options, ownership of intellectual property, tax treatment, contracts, liability, estate planning, beneficiary protections, trustee/executor roles, and provider transfer rules.

## Change control

Only the founder or a properly verified legally authorized successor may change this operating mode. Any change must be recorded with the date, approver, reason, and supporting legal/business decision. Agents may recommend a change but cannot make it.
